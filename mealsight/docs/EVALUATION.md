# MealSight evaluation: the standing record

Written for engineers who maintain MealSight and need to know what the evaluation suite measures, how far to trust
it, what it currently says, and where it is weak. It is the summary; per-run detail and the running history of
corrections live in [`backend/eval/README.md`](../backend/eval/README.md), and every run's full machine-readable
result is in `backend/eval/results/`.

**Current result (run_1794032750, 2026-11-07): overall FAIL.** Four of five dimensions pass. Dimension 1 (ingredient
recognition from photos) fails at 0.488 against its 0.60 threshold, and that failure is understood (see below).

---

## 1. What each dimension measures, and why

**Dimension 1: ingredient recognition.** From a photo of a fridge or pantry, does the vision step name what is actually
there? Scored as F1 (precision and recall, visibility-adjusted recall) against hand-labelled ground truth for 30 photos,
using a matcher that is deliberately strict about look-alikes (a scallion is not an onion, sweet potato is not
potato). Everything downstream depends on this: a recipe cannot be matched against a pantry the system misread.

**Dimension 2: constraint satisfaction.** Does the system ever recommend something that breaks what the user asked
for? 55 natural-language requests run through the real pipeline: dietary restrictions (alone and stacked), cook-time
limits, servings, avoid-ingredient and avoid-dish lists, cuisine preference, and requests that should be honestly
refused. Hard constraints (dietary, time, servings, avoid lists) are binary with no partial credit and a threshold of
1.0, because a dietary violation is a real harm rather than a quality miss. Cuisine preference is soft and scored
separately (threshold 0.50) on the cases where a hit was possible with this pantry. A satisfiable request that is
wrongly refused fails its case; a correct refusal passes.

**Dimension 3: match accuracy.** When the matcher reports a score, is it right? 32 hand-derived cases check the
numeric score and the per-ingredient classification (matched / substitutable / partial / missing) **separately**: a score
that lands right while two ingredients are misclassified fails. It covers exact and synonym matches, specificity in both
directions (chicken thighs for "chicken"; generic chicken for "chicken breast"), protected terms that must not collapse
(sweet potato, cream cheese, green/spring onion, hot sauce), substitutions including one rejected on dietary grounds,
critical-missing forcing `can_cook` false, long-expired pantry items excluded, avoid-list exclusion, and optional-only
gaps. No model calls.

**Dimension 4: freshness prioritization.** When an ingredient is near expiry, does the recommendation use it? An
expiring item that no cookable recipe uses is not a failure; a far better match correctly beating a marginal recipe that
uses the item is not a failure. Only "a cookable recipe that used it was possible, and the system neither used it nor was
correctly out-scored" fails. Items past the 2-day grace window are waste and must not be reported as opportunities.

**Dimension 5: personalization.** After a profile is seeded, do recommendations align with it? Learned cuisine
preferences, disliked ingredients, a stored protein preference, household size driving servings, preferred cook time as a
soft default, repetition avoidance against recent meals, and (added after the first runs) saved dietary restrictions.

## 2. Methodology

**Expectations are hand-derived, not generated by running the system.** For Dimension 3 every expected classification and
score was worked out by hand from the recipe's real ingredient rows, the full synonym and substitution tables, and the
matcher's documented rules, before the matcher was run on the case (the case file's hash was recorded before the first
run, and a test applies the documented formula to each case's own expected buckets to catch arithmetic slips). Dimensions
4 and 5 derive their expectations from the documented ranking terms; `eval/analysis.py` holds a separate, literal copy of
those weights so the evaluation is an independent check on the ranking rather than a mirror of it. Each case also states
its derivation. Where an expectation rests on what an ingredient means rather than on a documented rule it is marked
`basis="intent"` and reported separately.

**Fairness to the system.** The suite computes what was possible before judging what was done: it runs the matcher over
the whole 250-recipe corpus for each pantry to find the cookable recipes (Dimensions 4 and 5), and a case whose own design
does not produce the situation it claims is reported as a *case defect*, never as a system failure (this caught real
mistakes in the suite itself, listed in the eval README).

**Isolation.** Dimensions 4 and 5 put the system in an exact state per case. They run against throwaway pantry and profile
SQLite files in a temp directory (the three MCP servers are pointed at them through `MCPClientManager(env=...)`), recreated
from the real schema before every case and asserted to hold zero rows. Seeded state is read back through the servers' own
read functions and compared with what the case asked for. The real pantry, user and recipe databases are content-hashed
(every table's rows, so a WAL checkpoint does not raise a false alarm) before and after each dimension and must be
unchanged. Dimensions 1 and 2 run against the real seeded demo pantry (`scripts/reset_demo_pantry.py`, fixture
`756090ab287f`, 64 rows), reset before each full run.

**Mutation checks.** To confirm the Dimension 3 cases exercise the paths they claim, the matcher was mutated in thirteen ways
(no protected terms, weight 1.0 for substitutes, ignoring dietary restrictions, no critical penalty, longer grace, a higher
threshold, no specificity, no synonyms, avoid filter off, can_cook ignoring critical, and others) and each mutation had to
make cases fail. One did not (removing the protected-term list changed nothing), which exposed a weak case; it was replaced.

**Scorer verification.** Every scorer has known-answer fixture tests (a dietary violation fails; a correct refusal
passes; a score right with a misclassified ingredient fails; and so on). 936 tests cover the system and the suite.

**Provenance.** Every scorecard records: model ids; hashes of the three prompts; hashes of the lexicons and rule sets in
code (diet groups, dietary vocabulary, protein lexicon, cuisine families, avoid groups, restriction-detection patterns,
matcher normalization, matcher thresholds, ranking weights); hashes of the reference data read at run time (recipe corpus,
synonym, substitution and nutrition tables); the pantry fixture and a hash of its content; the git commit, whether the
tree was dirty, and a hash of the diff. **Limit:** the component hashes cover lexicons, prompts and data, not code
*logic*. A change to how `match_rank` uses a lexicon moves only the git diff hash.

**Thresholds** were fixed before the first run of each dimension and have not been adjusted: 1.0 for Dimensions 2 (hard)
and 3, 0.50 for the soft cuisine score, 0.60 for Dimension 1, and 0.80 for Dimensions 4 and 5.

## 4. Dimension 1's failure and its cause

Dimension 1 fails (0.488 against 0.60) and the cause is not a regression in the code. The original model, `mistral-medium-2505`,
scored a mean F1 of 0.64 and the 0.60 threshold was set against it. Mistral **retired that model**, and the medium tier (and
every medium, small and magistral model) is **unavailable on this account's free plan** (0 requests per minute; see
[`VISION_FINDINGS.md`](VISION_FINDINGS.md)). The best reachable replacement is `ministral-8b-2512`, which
scores about 0.49 to 0.55 depending on the scorer. Two scorer corrections made since the 0.64 figure (a photo where the model
identifies nothing now counts as a zero, and a scallion no longer matches an onion) can only lower a score, so 0.64 and 0.49
are not like-for-like. A prompt comparison on the replacement model (photos 1 to 5, three repetitions, temperature 0) found
the production prompt no worse than two alternatives (0.540 against 0.478 and 0.536), so the gap is the model tier, not the
wording. The threshold has not been lowered to fit the new model: the dimension is failing honestly, and it stays that
way until a stronger vision model is reachable.

## 5. Known gaps

These are open, measured, and deliberately not hidden by the passing scores above.

1. **The reasoning step can override a correct freshness ranking (Dimension 4, d4_13).** The ranking puts the recipe that
   uses the expiring butter first (Mince Pies, composite 0.775), then the language model chooses Pancakes as "the best available
   match" (0.675, no butter). It fails identically in every run. The same override produced two other failures earlier
   (d4_06, d4_09). The fix needs a decision about how much the final choice may depart from the ranking.
2. **`cooking_skill` and `budget_sensitivity` have no code effect.** They reach the model only as a line of prompt text. The
   learned protein scores in `preference_scores` are stored but not read by ranking, and `cuisine_preference_data_points` is
   not consumed (shrinkage is applied when scores are stored).
3. **Nutritional restrictions are disclosed, not enforced.** `low-carb`, `keto`, `low-sodium` and `low-fat` (and `paleo`) are
   answered with an explicit "NOT ENFORCED" statement instead of a filter. Measured over the whole corpus, the nutrition
   estimates are not reliable enough to filter on (a baked-potato dish is estimated at 13 g of carbohydrate per serving, a
   tomato soup at 241 g, sodium runs from 6 to 33,192 mg per serving). `halal` and `kosher` are partial by design: pork,
   alcohol, shellfish and meat-with-dairy are excluded, and the answer says that slaughter and certification cannot be verified.
4. **Provenance hashes do not cover code logic** (above); a behavioural change to ranking shows only in the git diff hash.
5. **Not covered by a dimension:** the multi-day meal planner (it received the same profile fixes and has unit tests but no
   scored cases), the `find_substitutions` tool (documented to ignore the user's diet), and the cook endpoint's use of the matcher.
6. **Operational quirk:** the evaluation process does not exit by itself after writing the scorecard (an open connection keeps
   it alive); it has to be stopped once "scorecard written" appears.

## 6. Caveats on the evidence

- **The pantry fixture is thin.** Only 22 of the 250 recipes are cookable from the seeded demo pantry, and in Dimension 2 about
  twenty of the thirty original recommendations came from the same six recipes. A pass is therefore a statement about this
  pantry, not about pantries in general; a richer fixture would exercise more of the system.
- **Dimension 2 was re-baselined after the fact.** Its first full run (run_1793926853) scored 20/30 with 0 violations and 10
  wrongly-refused requests. Three authored expectations (d2_10, d2_12, d2_30) were then found to be wrong against the pantry
  and were corrected, d2_06 (keto) changed by design when keto became a disclosed-but-unenforced restriction, and the pipeline
  changed too (profile cook time as a soft default, relaxation on "nothing cookable", ingredient-level restrictions). Each
  correction is verified, dated and documented in `eval/cases.py`, the original values are kept, and the first run's scorecard keeps the
  original expectations, but the move from 20/30 to 55/55 is not a blind result. Cases were also added after the first run
  (cuisine cases d2_31 to d2_43, restriction cases d2_44 to d2_55), each labelled attainable or not against the cookable list
  before it was run.
- **The vision benchmark and production use different prompts.** The benchmark behind the model choice used a pipe-delimited
  output format; production uses a JSON-extended variant of the same prompt. The two have not been compared directly, so the
  headline Dimension 1 figure measures a near neighbour of what ships (this is also noted in `eval/README.md`).
- **The final recommendation is a language-model choice**, so Dimensions 4 and 5 carry some run-to-run variance. Dimension 4's
  failing set varied across earlier runs ({d4_06, d4_09}, {d4_13}) before settling on d4_13 alone; Dimension 5 once passed d5_02 and
  d5_08 by tie-breaking (now reported as `passed_without_scoring_support`, which is empty in the final run).
- **Small samples.** 15 to 55 cases per dimension; a single case moves Dimension 4 by 0.067.
- **Some expectations rest on intent, not rules** (`basis="intent"` in Dimension 3: a garlic clove is garlic, a stock cube is stock).
  They are reported separately.
- **The suite tests one model on the free plan.** Results say nothing about a larger model.

## 7. Bugs the suite found

This is the strongest argument for having built it. Each was invisible to the existing unit tests, because the code involved
was individually correct and simply never called correctly, or called with the wrong data.

| Bug | Found by | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Avoid lists were unenforced.** `avoid_ingredients` and `avoid_dishes` reached the single-request pipeline only as prompt text; whether a nut dish was recommended to a nut-free request depended on the model obeying. | Dimension 2 | A nut allergy expressed as an avoid list excluded nothing in code. Now enforced by a whole-word matcher with allergen groups (nut, shellfish, dairy). |
| **`match_rank` discarded dietary restrictions.** The matcher refuses a substitute that breaks a restriction, but only if told the restriction; `match_rank` never passed it. | Dimension 3 | A dairy-free user with Greek yogurt in the pantry was offered it as a mayonnaise substitute. Now passed, including avoid lists and ingredient-enforced restrictions. |
| **Untagged restrictions were dead filters.** 12 of the 17 restrictions in the extraction vocabulary have no corpus tag, yet each was sent to search as a hard tag filter. | Dimension 2 audit | Any request carrying one (peanut-free, pescatarian, halal ...) matched zero recipes and was refused instead of protected. Now enforced against ingredient lists, or disclosed as not enforced. |
| **Profile restrictions were ignored entirely.** Saved `dietary_restrictions` were stored correctly and never merged into the request. | Dimension 5 probe | A profile with `vegetarian` got a salmon dish; a saved nut allergy would have been dropped the same way. Now unioned with the request and treated identically. |
| **Cuisine learning was dead in production.** `_cuisine_score` looked up the lower-cased cuisine against profile keys that keep their logged capitalization (`{'Italian': 0.885}` scored 0.5). | Dimension 5 | Learned cuisine preferences never affected a ranking outside tests. Fixed where scores are stored and read, using the cuisine families. |
| **Nutrition was about 4x too high.** Per-serving values were the base-recipe totals divided by the requested servings without first scaling to them, so one serving of a four-serving recipe reported roughly four times the real per-serving figure. | The evaluation work (recorded by the project owner; the mechanism is confirmed in the code change) | Corrected in `recipe_engine/nutrition.py` (scale to the requested servings, then divide); per-serving values no longer depend on how many servings were asked for. |
| **Garlic clove did not match garlic.** The matcher treated "garlic clove" as a different ingredient from "garlic". | Dimension 3 | 25 of 250 recipes (10%) under-scored a pantry that held garlic. Now a synonym; the spice "cloves" is untouched. |
| **Stock cube did not match stock (either direction).** | Dimension 3 | A pantry with stock cubes could not cook a recipe needing chicken stock (a critical ingredient), so `can_cook` was false. Now synonyms for the chicken and vegetable stock cubes. |

Also found and fixed along the way (details in `eval/README.md`): the profile's default cook time (30 minutes, never set by the
user) was enforced as a hard cap; the extraction model sometimes filed "nut-free" under `avoid_ingredients` instead of
`dietary_restrictions`, and a deterministic detector now backs the model up; the cuisine name "Indian" or "French" never matched the
corpus labels "India" and "France", so the system told a user no Indian recipe existed while one was cookable; the default-time
nudge was dropped for exactly the top candidates by the repetition re-score; stored dislikes were enforced only by the model
reading one prompt line; a stored protein preference could not reach the request; the freshness bonus was missed when the pantry
item was a more specific cut than the recipe line; and, in earlier phases, a pantry freshness-decay bug on re-sighting an item,
long-expired items still being offered to the matcher, and a protein-ranking gap. Twelve defects in the suite's own scorers were
also found and corrected during preparation.

## 7a. Provider free-tier change (2026-11-07)
After the final scorecard, the provider's free tier was restructured. Live `x-ratelimit` headers now give `ministral-8b-2512` **2 requests/minute and 40,000 tokens/minute** (the Phase 1 console reading, used throughout the evaluation work, was 3.13 RPS and 625,000 TPM). Whisper on Groq reports 2,000 requests/day in its headers; its 20 requests/minute figure is console-sourced and unverified. Consequences:
- The runs recorded here were made under the old, faster allowance; the scores are unchanged by the tier, but a full five-dimension run at the current allowance would need about an hour of rate-limit waiting (118 calls at one per 30 s) and cannot finish inside the CI job timeout. The scheduled nightly run was therefore removed (the workflow, `eval-full.yml`, remains for manual dispatch).
- Dimension 3 needs no API calls and still runs on every PR.
- Tokens are not the constraint: ~426,000 per full run is about 4 cents against the $10 monthly allowance, with pay-as-you-go disabled. Throughput is.
- Per-recommendation latency changed as well; see the README's operating characteristics.

## 8. Running it

```
# from backend/, with the demo pantry reset first for Dimensions 1 and 2
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python ../scripts/reset_demo_pantry.py
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python -m eval                 # all five dimensions
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python -m eval dimension3      # one or more by name
```

The scorecard is written to `backend/eval/results/run_<timestamp>.json`; stop the process once it prints "scorecard written".
Dimension 3 needs no network; the others call the free-tier Mistral API.
