2. FEATURE 1's PROTEIN VARIETY FORMULA: NORMALIZED SHANNON ENTROPY
   (PIELOU'S EVENNESS INDEX), NOT A DISTINCT-COUNT
=============================================================
protein_variety_score = (-sum(p * ln p) over each distinct protein's own
share p of protein-identifiable meals) / ln(N), where N is how many
distinct proteins appear at all. 0.0 means every meal centered on one
protein; 1.0 means every distinct protein appeared equally often.

Chosen and reported explicitly because the task named the exact failure
mode a plain distinct-count has: cooking chicken nine times and beef
once IS "2 distinct proteins," a naive count that hides a completely
skewed pattern. Verified directly, not just asserted: a real 9-chicken/
1-beef split scores well below a 5-chicken/5-beef split built from the
identical two proteins (test_protein_variety_scores_a_skewed_
distribution_lower_than_an_even_one), and a genuinely single-protein
history scores exactly 0.0 (a special case handled explicitly, since
ln(1)=0 would otherwise divide by zero).

Meals with no identifiable protein (a vegetable/grain-forward dish)
are excluded from the denominator entirely, not counted as their own
"protein" — a dish deriving no protein_type isn't evidence of dietary
variety, it's just absence of a signal.


3. FEATURE 2's DENOMINATOR: THE SUPPLIED LIST'S OWN SIZE, THE EXACT
   OPPOSITE OF _pantry_overlap_score's OWN DENOMINATOR
=============================================================
_pantry_overlap_score (search.py) answers "of THIS RECIPE's own
ingredients, how many are in the pantry" — matched / the recipe's own
ingredient count. That is precisely the wrong question for reverse
search, which answers "of the ingredients I GAVE YOU, how many did this
recipe actually use" — match_percentage = matched / the SUPPLIED list's
own size. The task's own worked example is a direct illustration of why
the denominator has to flip: a recipe using all 3 of 3 supplied
ingredients must outrank one using those same 3 ingredients out of its
own 12, and the old (recipe-sized) denominator would score the second
recipe at a punishing 3/12=25% purely for happening to have more
ingredients overall — a fact about the recipe that has nothing to do
with how well it used what was actually supplied.

Verified directly on real corpus data, not just in a synthetic test: a
targeted 8-ingredient query ranked a 4-ingredient recipe using all 4 of
its own ingredients (50% of supplied) above three recipes with 11, 14,
and 16 total ingredients of their own, all three of which matched 3 of
the same 8 supplied items (37.5% each) — under the OLD denominator
those three would have scored 3/14≈21%, 3/16≈19%, 3/11≈27% respectively,
each artificially depressed by the recipe's own size, a fact this tool's
whole existence is meant to correct for.

The canonicalization pipeline itself (normalize_ingredient +
resolve_canonical against one load_synonym_map call) is the literal
same code shape _pantry_overlap_score already uses — only the fraction's
own denominator differs, exactly matching the task's own "reuse that
logic" instruction read as "reuse the comparison, not the formula."


4. WASTE CORRELATION: settings.waste_insight_threshold REUSED, ONE
   NEW THRESHOLD ADDED WHERE NOTHING EXISTING ANSWERED IT
=============================================================
"Frequently wasted" reuses settings.waste_insight_threshold (phase
9.4's own constant) directly — there was no reason a second,
independently-tuned "has this been wasted enough to mention" threshold

tried a single time.

its own waste-correlation suggestion has to read pantry.db's waste_log
— a genuinely NEW third physical database this package's own tests now
reach into, following the identical "load into memory, never a SQL
join" discipline scoring.py/repetition.py already established for their
own recipes.db reads. tests/test_user_intelligence/conftest.py gained
a pantry_db fixture for exactly this; tests/test_mcp_servers/test_
user_intelligence/conftest.py's own _fresh_user_db fixture ALSO needed
extending to monkeypatch settings.pantry_db_path — caught directly
while writing the MCP-level tests: without it, a real MCP tool test for
get_taste_insights would have silently queried whatever real pantry.db
happens to exist on disk instead of a throwaway one.

Verified both directions: fires and names the real ingredient and real
count above threshold; does NOT fire below threshold; does NOT fire
when the ingredient is genuinely, frequently cooked with despite being
wasted often too (three separate, explicit tests, not one conflated
one).


5. A REAL, NAMED DUPLICATION LEFT UNFIXED — REPORTED, NOT SILENTLY
   PATCHED OVER
=============================================================
Phase 9.5's own meal_planner.py already has its own _protein_type
helper, deriving a recipe's protein a slightly different way (from
match_ingredients' own matched/missing/partial item lists, since
planning candidates never carry a full ingredients_by_recipe map the
way get_taste_insights' own meal_history rows do). This phase's own
get_taste_insights instead reuses scoring.py's derive_protein directly,
since it already has recipe_db loaded and the exact ingredient lists on
hand. Two real, independently-justified protein-derivation call sites
now exist in this codebase, doing conceptually the same thing slightly
differently for genuinely different reasons (one has full recipe
ingredient lists in hand already, one only has match_ingredients' own
matched/missing/partial buckets) — named here explicitly as a real
observation rather than silently reconciled, since unifying them would
have meant either giving meal_planner.py an extra get_recipe call per
candidate it doesn't otherwise need, or giving get_taste_insights a
dependency on match_ingredients it doesn't otherwise need — neither
was in scope for this phase's own task.

8. TWO REAL BUGS FOUND WHILE VERIFYING, BOTH FIXED BEFORE MOVING ON
=============================================================
FastAPI query-param inference: GET /api/recipes/by-ingredients' own
`ingredients: list[str]` parameter, with no default, was silently
inferred as a REQUIRED REQUEST BODY field rather than a query
parameter — a real FastAPI behavior (a required list-typed parameter
defaults to body inference unless explicitly marked), caught by an
actual 422 in the REST-level test, not assumed. Fixed with an explicit
Annotated[list[str], Query()] rather than adding a fake default just to
dodge the inference rule.

A stale SQLite WAL file silently reintroducing newer data during a
live-verification restore: after backing up user_intelligence.db,
seeding demo meal history, and later restoring the ORIGINAL backup via
a plain file copy, a fresh row-count check still showed 7 meals, not
the original 3 — the backup file itself, checked independently, really
did have 3. The actual cause: killing the backend's processes leaves a
`-wal` file with the newer, uncommitted-relative-to-the-backup writes
still on disk, and a bare `cp` of the main `.db` file alone doesn't
touch it — the next real read replays that stale WAL right back on top
of the restored file. Fixed by deleting the `-wal`/`-shm` files
alongside the `.db` file itself before restoring, once every process
holding a connection was confirmed stopped. Recorded here as a real
addition to this project's own established "back up, confirm no live
connection, verify with an independent query" safe-mutation discipline
— that discipline needs one more step for a WAL-mode database
specifically: clear the WAL/SHM files too, or a plain file-copy restore
can silently fail to actually restore anything.

10. DECISIONS AND ALTERNATIVES REJECTED
=============================================================
A fresh, independently-tuned "frequently wasted" threshold for the
waste-correlation suggestion versus reusing settings.waste_insight_
threshold
    Rejected a second threshold: the underlying question ("has this
    been wasted enough times to be worth mentioning") is identical to
    the one phase 9.4's own waste_insight_threshold already answers —
    inventing a second, separately-tuned number for the same judgment
    asked from a different module would only risk the two drifting
    apart over time for no real benefit.

RARELY_COOKED_MAX_MEALS = 0 (never cooked with at all) versus 1 (tried
at most once)
    Rejected requiring zero: an ingredient bought once, genuinely tried
    in one real meal, and then repeatedly wasted afterward is still a
    real, worth-surfacing pattern ("you tried this once and it hasn't
    stuck") — requiring literal zero uses would have silently excluded
    exactly that case.

Reusing meal_planner.py's own _protein_type helper (phase 9.5) for
get_taste_insights versus reusing scoring.py's derive_protein instead
    Rejected importing across from the agent layer: get_taste_insights
    already has recipe_db loaded with full ingredient lists on hand
    (via load_recipe_ingredient_names, the same batch-load pattern
    scoring.py/repetition.py already use) — reaching into mealsight.
    agent.meal_planner for a differently-shaped helper built around
    match_ingredients' own item buckets would have been backwards, not
    simpler. The resulting duplication is real and named (section 5),
    not swept under the rug, but reconciling it was out of this
    phase's own scope.

Giving the reverse-search tool its own separate ingredient-matching
implementation versus reusing search.py's canonicalization pipeline
    Rejected a third implementation: the task's own explicit
    instruction named this exact risk, and _pantry_overlap_score's own
    normalize_ingredient + resolve_canonical + synonym_map pipeline was
    already the established, tested way this project decides "is this
    the same ingredient" — only the fraction's own denominator needed
    to differ, not the comparison underneath it.

Restoring a WAL-mode database from backup via a plain file copy alone
versus also clearing the WAL/SHM files
    Rejected the plain-copy-only approach once it was caught failing
    for real: a stale WAL file left behind after killing a database's
    own connections silently reintroduces newer writes on the next
    real read, making a "restored" backup not actually restored at
    all — an addition to this project's own established safe-mutation
    discipline, not a one-off fix.

12. INTERVIEW TALKING POINTS
=============================================================
Q: Your own verification found get_recipe_by_ingredients returning
ZERO results against the real, full pantry. Doesn't that mean the
feature doesn't actually work?

    No — it means the feature is working exactly as documented against
    a demanding, honestly-set default. minimum_match_percentage=0.6
    means a recipe has to use 60% of whatever ingredient list it's
    given; against a real 27-item pantry, that means matching roughly
    16 ingredients in one single recipe, which essentially no real
    recipe does — most dishes use a small, specific subset of a full
    kitchen's inventory. The SAME tool, run against a smaller, targeted
    list (the 8 ingredients actually relevant to a specific meal idea),
    correctly ranked real recipes by proportional use. The full-pantry
    result is a real, informative finding about how this default
    threshold behaves at that scale, not a failure — and reporting it
    honestly, rather than quietly re-running with a lower threshold
    until something looked better, was the more useful thing to hand
    back.

Q: You found a stale WAL file undid your first restore attempt. How do
you know your SECOND restore actually worked, rather than just getting
lucky?

    Because I didn't trust the file copy alone the second time either —
    I independently queried the restored database directly afterward
    (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM meal_history) and confirmed it matched the
    real, original 3-row count, the same "verify with a fresh,
    independent query" discipline this project already uses for every
    other database mutation. The first restore attempt looked identical
    from the outside (the cp command succeeded, no error) — the only
    way the discrepancy surfaced at all was checking the actual data
    afterward instead of trusting that a successful copy meant a
    successful restore.

Q: Why does the protein-variety formula treat a dish with no
identifiable protein as "absence of a signal" rather than its own
category (like "vegetarian")?

    Because folding it in as its own protein-like category would
    quietly change what the score measures. The formula's whole job is
    to say how evenly someone's ANIMAL-OR-PLANT-PROTEIN-FORWARD cooking
    is spread across real proteins (chicken, beef, tofu, and so on) —
    counting "no identifiable protein" as if it were one more entry in
    that same distribution would reward someone who mostly cooks
    protein-free dishes with an artificially high variety score, even
    though they're not actually varying their PROTEIN choices at all,
    they're just not making many protein-centered choices in the first
    place. Excluding those meals from the denominator entirely keeps
    the score honestly measuring what it claims to measure.
